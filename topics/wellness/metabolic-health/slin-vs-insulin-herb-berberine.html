Put the serving units beside the number

SLIN lists 500 mg berberine HCl per four-capsule manufacturer serving. The newly reviewed Insulin Herb panel lists 500 mg berberine HCl per one vegetarian capsule. These use the same named ingredient form but different serving denominators. They are not a personal dose-for-dose substitution, and a higher per-capsule amount does not establish a better result.

The Insulin Herb panel now resolves a gap in our earlier guide: it lists milk thistle seed extract 105 mg, standardized to at least 80% silymarin, and piperine 5 mg. SLIN presents a wider multi-botanical formula, but its separate affiliate bundle still mentions Gymnema, absent from the current manufacturer table. Neither list constitutes an independent assay.

The bottle price does not describe the linked order

Our SLIN guide leads to a six-bottle offer. Its separate manufacturer store advertised US$45.99 for one bottle, which is a different purchasing route. Comparing that store figure with the affiliate bundle without naming the quantity would hide the larger upfront commitment.

The Insulin Herb guide records one- and three-bottle prices and conflicting shipping language. Its sales page describes a 30-day bottle while also using two-to-three-capsule directions. Treat those supply claims as unresolved arithmetic, not instructions to take two or three capsules. Request the actual bottle label and an address-specific quote.

What to settle before choosing

Ask SLIN for the formula attached to the exact affiliate bundle and resolve whether Gymnema belongs to that version. For Insulin Herb, compare the newly available full panel with the physical package and its shared-equipment allergen warning. Both complete formulas, rather than just their berberine entry, belong in a suitability review.

Neither a product name nor seller copy justifies changing diabetes care or combining a supplement with a prescription medicine. Take the full ingredient lists to a qualified clinician or pharmacist.

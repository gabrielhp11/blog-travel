Label

Eleven ingredient names share one 500 mg amount

The supplier wrapper defines a serving as one capsule and lists thirty servings. Its proprietary blend combines wild yam, rehmannia, dodder seed, eucommia, poria, achyranthes, cistanche, polygala, schisandra, cornus and cnidium. The total belongs to the mixture, not to each botanical.

Standardization and extract ratios describe particular raw materials; they do not reveal the missing amount of each ingredient. Gelatin also matters when comparing vegetarian capsule products. Check the actual bottle’s complete warnings before considering suitability.

Delivery: confirm the actual destination quote

The two-bottle card adds $9.99, while the general shipping table says US shipping is free. That inconsistency needs an order-specific answer. The page lists longer international estimates; it does not confirm delivery to every country or a final tax amount.

Returns: check the deadline and the procedure

The detailed policy starts a 60-day period at delivery, requires all bottles and the packing slip, and makes the customer pay return postage. It also suggests waiting three months, which extends beyond sixty days. Do not let that suggestion make you miss a refund deadline; obtain written instructions promptly.

A blend label does not validate the sales narrative

The sales page links digestive conditions to lead and promises a cleansing mechanism. The reviewed wrapper does not establish those claims, and PokiSky has not independently tested the product. Persistent digestive symptoms need qualified assessment. This formula should not be treated as a substitute for diagnosis, prescribed treatment or investigation of a suspected exposure.

Questions before choosing

Does each ingredient provide 500 mg?

No. The panel gives 500 mg for the entire proprietary blend and does not disclose individual botanical amounts.

Does six bottles mean six months are suitable?

No. Pack size is a commercial choice, not an assessment of medical suitability or duration.

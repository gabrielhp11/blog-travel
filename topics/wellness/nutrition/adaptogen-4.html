60 capsules do not always mean two months

The commercial page presents a 60-capsule bottle as a two-month supply, while the label describes one capsule once or twice daily. Simple quantity arithmetic gives 60 days at one capsule or 30 days at two. This comparison explains the count; it does not recommend either schedule. The ingredient photograph below is supplier context artwork, not proof of the contents of a delivered capsule.

Warnings and suitability

The label restricts the product to adults and asks for professional advice with pregnancy, nursing or a known medical condition. Review every botanical and medication with a qualified health professional. Its manufacturing-facility statement mentions possible processing of common allergens. The printed California Proposition 65 lead warning should also be read; it is a label warning, not an independent measurement supplied by PokiSky. The guide does not establish weight loss, hormone changes or treatment for stress-related symptoms.

Returns: read the conditions behind the guarantee

The sales page uses broad 60-day guarantee wording. The detailed supplier policy adds conditions: it says to use as directed for 30 days before claiming, deducts applicable original shipping and handling, requires customer-paid return postage and a return number, and normally refunds at most one opened bottle plus returned unopened bottles. It limits repeat claims and gives additional damaged-package instructions. These are policy descriptions, not advice to continue a product that may be unsuitable.
